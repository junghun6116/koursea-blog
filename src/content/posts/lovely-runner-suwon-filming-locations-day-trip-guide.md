# Lovely Runner Filming Locations in Suwon: A Haenggung-dong Day Trip Guide

"Lovely Runner" (선재 업고 튀어) turned a quiet corner of Suwon's Haenggung-dong into one of the most searched K-drama filming neighborhoods among international fans — and unlike a studio set, it's a real, walkable district built around a UNESCO World Heritage fortress, not just a backdrop. This guide covers what's actually confirmed about the filming spots, how to get there from Seoul, where to stay, and the one house you should only look at from across the street.

For the broader context of Korean drama tourism, Koursea's [10 K-Drama & K-Pop Filming Locations Near Seoul](/posts/kdrama-kpop-filming-locations-seoul-2026/) covers other verified sites; this guide goes deep on just one neighborhood.

## What's Actually Confirmed

Korean entertainment and travel press (Kyeonggi.com, Dailian, WikiTree) consistently place the cafe where Lim Sol and Ryu Seon-jae first meet at a second-floor address on Hwaseomun-ro in Haenggung-dong, now operating as a cafe commonly referred to as Monte-d. Its signature items are reported as salt bread and strawberry cake. Reported operating hours and a walk-in-only (no reservation) policy vary by source and some information online dates to 2024 — treat any specific hours you see as unconfirmed until you check the cafe's own Instagram or a live Naver Map listing on the day you plan to go.

> **Practical rule:** Directly across the street from the cafe is the house used as Ryu Seon-jae's home in the drama. It is a real private residence with a resident living there — not a set. Do not approach the gate, photograph through windows, or linger. Admire the street from where you're standing and move on.

The wider Haenggung-dong/Haengridan-gil area — the name blends Seoul's Itaewon-adjacent "Gyeongridan-gil" with Haenggung-dong — has drawn drama and film productions for years precisely because of its hanok-lined alleys and the fortress backdrop; Hwaseong Fortress and Haenggung Palace themselves are described in Korean press as a recurring filming location for historical dramas and films generally, separate from this one drama's confirmed spots.

## Getting There from Seoul

Suwon Station is served directly by KTX and ITX-Saemaeul trains from Seoul Station, and by **Seoul Subway Line 1**, which runs straight through from central Seoul with no transfer. From Suwon Station, Haenggung-dong and Hwaseong Fortress are a further bus or taxi ride — the station itself is not walking distance from the fortress area. Confirm the current timetable on Korail or your subway app on the day you travel rather than assuming a fixed journey time, since express and local Line 1 services differ.

## Where to Stay

For a single day trip, most visitors base themselves in Seoul and travel to Suwon and back via Line 1 or KTX. If you'd rather stay overnight, lodging clusters near Suwon Station itself (better transit connections onward) and within walking distance of Haenggung-dong (closer to the fortress, cafes, and night views, but fewer large hotel options). Koursea's [Suwon Haenggung-dong Cafe Street guide](https://www.koursea.com/suwon-haenggung-dong-cafe-street-hanok-courtyards-pastries.html) covers the immediate neighborhood in more depth if you're staying inside it.

## Hwaseong Fortress and Haenggung Palace

Hwaseong Fortress is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and Haenggung Palace inside it runs standard daytime visiting hours with a modest entry fee, plus a separate combined ticket covering the palace and nearby museums. Suwon has also run a seasonal **night-opening** schedule on specific weekend and holiday evenings in past years, with different hours and a separate admission structure from the daytime visit. Because exact fees, night-season dates, and children's pricing have varied across the sources Koursea checked for this guide — and because seasonal schedules change year to year — confirm the current admission and night-opening calendar directly through Suwon's own tourism channels before building a trip around it. Koursea's [Hwaseong Fortress Night Tour guide](https://www.koursea.com/hwaseong-fortress-night-tour-illuminated-ramparts-secret-night-gates.html) covers the illuminated night-walk experience in detail.

## Food and Cafes Beyond Monte-d

Haengridan-gil has grown into a cafe-and-hanok-alley district independent of any one drama, so build in time beyond the one filming-location stop. Koursea's dedicated [cafe street guide](https://www.koursea.com/suwon-haenggung-dong-cafe-street-hanok-courtyards-pastries.html) lists hanok-courtyard cafes and specialty pastry shops in the immediate area if Monte-d has a wait.

## Summary Checklist

- [ ] Confirm Monte-d's current hours and walk-in/reservation policy on the day you go, not from an old blog post.
- [ ] View the house across the street respectfully — it's a private residence, not a photo stop.
- [ ] Take Line 1, KTX, or ITX to Suwon Station, then a bus or taxi into Haenggung-dong.
- [ ] Check Suwon's official tourism channel for the current Hwaseong Fortress/Haenggung Palace hours, fees, and any active night-opening season before you plan around it.
- [ ] Budget time for the wider Haengridan-gil cafe street, not just the one filming spot.
