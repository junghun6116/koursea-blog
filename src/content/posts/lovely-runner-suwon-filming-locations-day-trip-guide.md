# A Suwon Day Trip: Hwaseong Fortress, Haenggung-dong & Lovely Runner's Filming Cafe

Suwon's Haenggung-dong neighborhood, built around the UNESCO World Heritage Hwaseong Fortress, is a genuine day trip from Seoul on its own — and it also happens to hold the real cafe used in "Lovely Runner" (선재 업고 튀어). The photo above shows the fortress itself as general area context; it is not a still from the drama. This guide focuses on what's actually sourced: the fortress's official hours and fees, how to get there, where to stay, and the one filming-related house you should only ever look at from across the street.

For other sourced filming locations around Seoul, see Koursea's [10 K-Drama & K-Pop Filming Locations Near Seoul](/posts/kdrama-kpop-filming-locations-seoul-2026/). This guide goes deep on a single day trip instead.

## The Lovely Runner Connection, and Its Limits

Korean entertainment outlet [TF News reported a wave of fans visiting and photographing Haenggung-dong filming spots](https://news.tf.co.kr/read/ogmeta/2099841.htm) after "Lovely Runner" aired, and [WikiTree's roundup of Suwon drama-filming hotspots](https://www.wikitree.co.kr/articles/1028829) names a cafe in the same neighborhood as a key stop. Fan-community posts, including one on [fanmaum.com](https://fanmaum.com/community/Lovely-Runner/45700353), place that cafe — commonly referred to as Monte-d — at an address on Hwaseomun-ro in Haenggung-dong. None of these is the cafe's own official page, so treat the exact address, floor, and opening hours as unconfirmed until you check a live map listing or the venue's own social account.

> **Do not visit the house.** The same fan-community reporting describes a house directly across the street from the cafe, used on-screen as a character's home, as a real private residence with someone actually living there. Do not approach the gate, photograph through windows, or linger. Look at the street from where you're standing and keep moving.

Beyond this one cafe, Koursea could not independently confirm which specific scenes, if any, were filmed inside Hwaseong Fortress or Haenggung Palace themselves for this drama. Treat the fortress as the day trip's main destination in its own right, not as an extension of the filming-location stop.

## Getting There from Seoul

Suwon Station is served by KTX and ITX-Saemaeul trains departing Seoul Station, and by Seoul Subway Line 1. From Suwon Station, Haenggung-dong and Hwaseong Fortress require a further bus or taxi ride — the station is not within walking distance of the fortress area. This guide does not state a specific travel time or fare, because schedules and express/local service patterns change: confirm the current timetable directly with Korail or your subway/map app on the day you travel.

## Where to Stay

For a single day trip, most visitors base themselves in Seoul and travel to Suwon and back the same day via Line 1 or KTX. If you'd rather stay overnight, lodging clusters near Suwon Station itself (better onward transit) and within walking distance of Haenggung-dong (closer to the fortress and the cafe street, but fewer large hotel options). Koursea's [Suwon Haenggung-dong Cafe Street guide](https://www.koursea.com/suwon-haenggung-dong-cafe-street-hanok-courtyards-pastries.html) covers the immediate neighborhood if you're staying inside it.

## Hwaseong Fortress and Haenggung Palace: Sourced Hours and Fees

Suwon's own visitor-information pages ([seqNo=74](https://www.suwon.go.kr/web/visitsuwon/hs02/pages.do?seqNo=74), [seqNo=60](https://www.suwon.go.kr/web/visitsuwon/hs02/pages.do?seqNo=60)) list Haenggung Palace's daytime hours as 09:00–18:00 from March through October and 09:00–17:00 from November through February, with admission at 2,000 KRW for adults, 1,500 KRW for youth and military, and 1,000 KRW for children (lower group rates apply for 20 or more visitors). These are the figures as read from Suwon's own pages at this guide's verification date — city fee and hour schedules can still change, so confirm the live listing before you go.

Separately, a travel blog ([trvlogue.com](https://www.trvlogue.com/ko/suwon-haenggung-night-opening)) describes a seasonal **night-opening** period for Haenggung Palace running roughly May through early November on weekend and holiday evenings, with different hours and admission from the daytime visit. Koursea has not confirmed this against Suwon's own official page, so treat the night-opening dates, hours, and fee as unconfirmed until you check directly with the Suwon Cultural Foundation or Suwon's tourism office. Koursea's [Hwaseong Fortress Night Tour guide](https://www.koursea.com/hwaseong-fortress-night-tour-illuminated-ramparts-secret-night-gates.html) covers the illuminated night-walk experience itself in more detail.

If your interest is a different drama's Suwon filming spots, Koursea separately covers [Extraordinary Attorney Woo's filming locations](https://www.koursea.com/extraordinary-attorney-woo-filming-spots-suwon-ginkgo-tree-kimbap-shop.html) — a different part of the city, not inside Haenggung-dong.

## Food and Cafes Beyond Monte-d

Haengridan-gil — a nickname [Dailian's travel feature](https://www.dailian.co.kr/news/view/1476852/) traces to a blend of Seoul's "Gyeongridan-gil" and "Haenggung-dong" — has grown into a cafe-and-hanok-alley district on its own, independent of any one drama. Koursea's dedicated [cafe street guide](https://www.koursea.com/suwon-haenggung-dong-cafe-street-hanok-courtyards-pastries.html) lists hanok-courtyard cafes and pastry shops in the immediate area if Monte-d has a wait.
