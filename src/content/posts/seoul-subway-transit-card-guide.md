# The Ultimate Seoul Transit Guide: T-Money, Climate Card, and Subways Explained

Seoul boasts one of the most efficient, punctual, and cleanest public transportation systems in the world. With over 20 subway lines, hundreds of interconnected bus routes, and regional rail networks connecting directly into neighboring cities, getting around without a car is exceptionally easy.

However, international visitors frequently run into payment bottlenecks, confusion between competing transit pass systems, and strict transfer rules.

Here is the definitive guide to conquering Seoul’s public transit network seamlessly.

## 1. Choosing Your Card: T-Money vs. Climate Card vs. WOWPASS

Before boarding your first train, you must pick the right payment mechanism. Credit cards without local Korean transit chips cannot simply be tapped at turnstiles.

- **Standard T-Money Card:**
  - **Best For:** Most short-stay tourists planning a flexible itinerary across multiple cities (Seoul, Busan, Jeju).
  - **How It Works:** A reloadable prepaid transit card. The physical card costs around 3,000 to 5,000 KRW. You must reload it using **physical Korean cash** at convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) or station vending machines.
  - **Coverage:** Valid nationwide on subways, city buses, and even inside taxis.
- **Climate Card (기후동행카드 - Tourist Pass):**
  - **Best For:** Heavy transit users staying inside Seoul for 1 to 7 days.
  - **Options:** Available in 1-day (5,000 KRW), 2-day (8,000 KRW), 3-day (10,000 KRW), and 5-day (15,000 KRW) versions.
  - **Limitation:** Only valid on Seoul-area subways and Seoul municipal buses. It is **not** valid on the Shinbundang Line or transit systems outside the Seoul capital boundary (such as Incheon or Suwon).
- **WOWPASS:**
  - **Best For:** Travelers wanting an all-in-one debit card with an integrated T-Money chip. You can exchange foreign currency directly into KRW via dedicated airport/hotel kiosks, but note that the T-Money transit balance must still be reloaded separately with cash.

## 2. The Golden Rule of Free Transfers

Seoul uses an integrated distance-based fare structure. You can transfer between subways and buses up to **4 times** without paying a new base fare, provided you follow these rules:

1. **Always Tag In and Tag Out:** Tap your card when entering the subway or boarding a bus, and **always tap again when exiting**. Forgetting to tap out on a bus cancels your transfer discount and charges the maximum distance penalty.
2. **The 30-Minute Window:** Your transfer must occur within **30 minutes** of tapping out from the previous vehicle (extended to 60 minutes between 9:00 PM and 7:00 AM).
3. **No Same-Route Transfers:** Transferring between two buses of the exact same route number does not qualify as a discounted transfer.

## 3. Navigating Seoul Subway Stations Like a Pro

- **Look at Station Numbers, Not Just Names:** Every subway station has an assigned code (e.g., Gangnam Station is 222 on Line 2). Following the numbers on the platform floor makes it easy to confirm whether you are boarding a train heading in the correct direction.
- **Pay Attention to Fast Transfer Marks:** Platforms have car and door numbers painted on the ground (e.g., 5-3). Transit apps indicate the exact car number to board for the shortest flight of stairs or escalator during transfers.
- **Help Bell for Wrong Platforms:** If you swipe your card and realize you entered the wrong platform direction on an older line without an internal overpass, look for the wheelchair gate. Press the **Help Bell** on the side; station staff will immediately unlock the gate so you can cross over without being charged twice.
- **Express Trains vs. All-Stop Trains:** Lines 9 and the AREX (Airport Railroad) operate both Express (급행) and All-Stop (일반) trains on identical tracks. Check the digital overhead monitors—Express trains skip minor stations, which can cut travel times in half.

## 4. Key Rules of Etiquette

- **Reserved Seating:** The brightly marked seats at the ends of subway cars are strictly reserved for the elderly, disabled, and pregnant women. Even on packed trains, locals leave these seats open.
- **Keep Luggage Secure:** When traveling with rolling suitcases during rush hour (8:00–9:00 AM and 6:00–7:00 PM), keep bags tucked tightly between your feet to allow smooth passenger movement.
- **No Open Beverages:** By municipal regulation, passengers holding uncovered hot coffees, cups with straws, or unsealed food containers are barred from boarding public buses. Finish your drink before hopping on.
