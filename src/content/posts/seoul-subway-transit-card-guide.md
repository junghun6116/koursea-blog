# Seoul Transit Cards 2026: T-Money vs Climate Card

Seoul's subway and bus network is extensive, but the payment products do different jobs. A T-Money card charges each ride, the short-term Climate Card covers eligible Seoul transport for a fixed period, and WOWPASS combines a payment wallet with a separate T-Money transit balance. Choosing from the logo alone can leave you with the wrong coverage or an empty transit balance.

This guide uses Seoul Metropolitan Government information checked on September 28, 2026. Fares, eligible lines, vending-machine payment methods, and pass products can change, so verify the linked live pages before travel.

## 1. Choosing Your Card: T-Money vs. Climate Card vs. WOWPASS

Before boarding your first train, you must pick the right payment mechanism. Credit cards without local Korean transit chips cannot simply be tapped at turnstiles.

- **Standard T-Money card:**
  - **Best For:** Most short-stay tourists planning a flexible itinerary across multiple cities (Seoul, Busan, Jeju).
  - **How It Works:** A reloadable stored-value transit card. Card prices vary by design and seller, and value is deducted by use. Seoul's guide lists convenience stores and metropolitan-area station service centers as retail locations.
  - **Coverage:** Widely used on participating subways and buses and at other T-Money merchants, but the exact route or vehicle must still accept it.
- **Climate Card short-term tourist pass (기후동행카드):**
  - **Best For:** Heavy transit users staying inside Seoul for 1 to 7 days.
  - **Options:** The official prices are 1 day ₩5,000, 2 days ₩8,000, 3 days ₩10,000, 5 days ₩15,000, and 7 days ₩20,000. The physical card is purchased separately.
  - **Important timing rule:** The short-term validity period begins when the card is charged; it cannot be loaded in advance for a later travel date.
  - **Limitation:** Coverage is route-specific. It does not simply mean every train or bus inside the capital region is included. Use the official coverage search for airport routes, privately operated lines, and trips extending beyond Seoul.
- **WOWPASS:**
  - **Best For:** Travelers wanting a prepaid payment wallet and an integrated T-Money function on one physical card.
  - **Important:** The payment wallet and T-Money transit value are separate balances. Loading the payment wallet does not automatically fund the transit side.

> **2026 product change:** Seoul discontinued recharging the old 30-day prepaid Climate Card after August 31, 2026 and launched a resident-focused Climate Pass. The short-term 1-, 2-, 3-, 5-, and 7-day tourist passes continue. Visitors should not confuse the resident Climate Pass with the tourist short-term pass.

## Quick decision table

| Your trip | Usually the better starting point | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seoul plus Busan, Jeju, Incheon, or Suwon | T-Money | Stored value is more flexible across regions and operators |
| Several eligible Seoul rides per day for 1–7 days | Climate Card short-term pass | Fixed-period travel can be convenient if every planned route is covered |
| Shopping payment wallet plus transit on one card | WOWPASS with T-Money balance | One physical card, but two balances that must be managed separately |
| One or two subway rides | T-Money or single-journey tickets | A time pass may not recover its purchase price |

List your airport transfer and every out-of-Seoul day before choosing. A pass that fails on one expensive route can erase the expected savings.

## 2. How Transfer Discounts Work

Seoul uses an integrated distance-based fare structure. The official rules allow up to **four transfers, or five boardings**, when the trip and card use qualify. This is a transfer discount, not a promise that every next ride costs zero; distance charges and the highest applicable base fare can still apply.

1. **Always Tag In and Tag Out:** Tap your card when entering the subway or boarding a bus, and **always tap again when exiting**. Forgetting to tap out on a bus cancels your transfer discount and charges the maximum distance penalty.
2. **The 30-Minute Window:** Your transfer must occur within **30 minutes** of tapping out from the previous vehicle (extended to 60 minutes between 9:00 PM and 7:00 AM).
3. **No Same-Route Transfers:** Transferring between two buses of the exact same route number does not qualify as a discounted transfer.
4. **Use One Card Consistently:** The system must be able to connect the rides. Multi-passenger handling has additional restrictions, and subway transfers do not support multi-passenger boarding in the same way as buses.

## 3. Navigating Seoul Subway Stations Like a Pro

- **Look at Station Numbers, Not Just Names:** Every subway station has an assigned code (e.g., Gangnam Station is 222 on Line 2). Following the numbers on the platform floor makes it easy to confirm whether you are boarding a train heading in the correct direction.
- **Pay Attention to Fast Transfer Marks:** Platforms have car and door numbers painted on the ground (e.g., 5-3). Transit apps indicate the exact car number to board for the shortest flight of stairs or escalator during transfers.
- **Help Bell for Wrong Platforms:** If you swipe your card and realize you entered the wrong platform direction on an older line without an internal overpass, look for the wheelchair gate. Press the **Help Bell** on the side; station staff will immediately unlock the gate so you can cross over without being charged twice.
- **Express Trains vs. All-Stop Trains:** Lines 9 and the AREX (Airport Railroad) operate both Express (급행) and All-Stop (일반) trains on identical tracks. Check the digital overhead monitors—Express trains skip minor stations, which can cut travel times in half.

Seoul's official fare page lists the adult subway card base fare at ₩1,550 as of June 28, 2025, with distance surcharges after the base range. Treat that as a dated reference, not a guaranteed future fare. Check the station display or official page for the fare on your travel date.

## 4. Key Rules of Etiquette

- **Reserved Seating:** The brightly marked seats at the ends of subway cars are strictly reserved for the elderly, disabled, and pregnant women. Even on packed trains, locals leave these seats open.
- **Keep Luggage Secure:** When traveling with rolling suitcases during rush hour (8:00–9:00 AM and 6:00–7:00 PM), keep bags tucked tightly between your feet to allow smooth passenger movement.
- **No Open Beverages on buses:** Seoul's restriction cited here applies to public buses: drivers can refuse uncovered hot drinks, cups with straws, and other spill-prone open containers. Finish or securely seal the drink before boarding.

## Buying and loading with an overseas card

Do not assume every machine accepts the same payment method. Seoul says physical Climate Cards and short-term passes can be purchased or recharged with international credit or debit cards at supported new station kiosks. That does not mean every T-Money reload point, convenience store, or older machine accepts an overseas card.

Keep a small Korean-won cash backup until your transit card is working. If a machine rejects the card, look for the international-card logo, try an official station service point, or ask staff rather than repeatedly triggering bank fraud controls.
