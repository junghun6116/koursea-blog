# Potenza Microneedling in Korea 2026: Cost Guide

Potenza is a radiofrequency microneedling platform. Arrays of fine electrodes enter the skin and deliver RF energy at selected depths, creating controlled mechanical and thermal effects. Clinics market it for concerns including texture, pores, atrophic acne scars, and skin laxity, but the device name alone does not define the treatment you will receive.

Needle tip, depth, energy, pulse mode, number of passes, treatment area, clinician technique, and combination products can all change the result, downtime, risk, and price. This guide shows foreign visitors how to compare a quote without mistaking “Potenza” for one standardized protocol.

## What Potenza Actually Treats

RF microneedling differs from both an injectable skin booster and a pigment-targeting laser. The needles mechanically enter the skin while RF energy produces localized heat at selected depths. The proposed plan should therefore match the concern and anatomy rather than using one generic setting for every face.

Systematic reviews suggest RF microneedling can improve atrophic acne scars and other selected concerns, but studies use different devices and protocols. Evidence about one indication cannot automatically be transferred to every “pore,” “tightening,” or rejuvenation package sold by a clinic.

Active inflammatory acne, infection, a history of abnormal scarring, recent procedures, medications, implanted devices, pregnancy, bleeding risk, and skin sensitivity may affect suitability. Give the treating clinician a complete history.

## Factory-Style vs. Private Clinic

A high-volume clinic may offer lower pricing, standardized intake, faster turnover, and rotating clinicians. A private clinic may provide longer consultation time and greater continuity with one doctor. Those are service-model tendencies, not guarantees of outcome.

It is inaccurate to say that the entire price gap buys only “the same doctor.” RF microneedling results and risks can be affected by diagnosis, tip selection, needle depth, energy, pulse mode, number of passes, treatment zone, tissue thickness, and operator judgment. Included anesthesia, aftercare, combination treatments, and follow-up also affect price.

Ask who will examine you, who will perform the procedure, and whether that person can change the plan after seeing your skin. Confirm that your consultation notes and requested settings reach the actual treating clinician.

## The Tip-and-Settings Comparison Trap

Two full-face Potenza sessions can be different products in practical terms. One may use a specific insulated or non-insulated tip and multiple passes; another may use a different tip, fewer passes, or only a limited zone. Some packages add topical products or another treatment, which creates a different risk and price profile.

The clinic may not finalize energy or depth before examining you, which is reasonable. It should still explain what the advertised package includes and which variables are decided during consultation.

## FDA Safety Warning: RF Microneedling Is Not a Spa Treatment

In October 2025, the U.S. FDA warned patients and clinicians about reports of serious complications with certain uses of RF microneedling devices. Reported problems included burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement, nerve damage, and cases requiring medical or surgical intervention.

The FDA recommends choosing a licensed healthcare provider trained and experienced with RF microneedling and asking which device will be used. It also states that RF microneedling is a medical procedure and should not be performed with at-home devices.

Regulatory authorization differs by country and intended use. Ask the Korean clinic to identify the exact console and disposable tip and explain how it verifies authentic supplies. Do not assume an English advertisement or a familiar brand name proves that a specific protocol is appropriate.

## Downtime and Aftercare

Koursea uses a typical **one-to-three-day** planning window for visible redness and sensitivity after many sessions. Some clinic protocols advise avoiding makeup for 24–48 hours and strenuous exercise, sauna, or high heat for 48–72 hours. These are general planning ranges, not universal instructions.

Deeper treatment, higher energy, multiple passes, acne-scar protocols, combination products, or individual sensitivity can produce longer redness, swelling, crusting, or post-inflammatory pigment changes. Follow the written instructions from the clinician who knows the actual settings used.

Use a gentle cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen only as directed. Do not restart retinoids, acids, scrubs, or other active products based on a generic online timeline.

## Red Flags After Treatment

Contact a licensed healthcare provider promptly if pain, redness, swelling, numbness, weakness, blistering, drainage, discoloration, or heat is severe or worsening rather than settling. Seek urgent assessment for signs of burns, infection, tissue injury, nerve symptoms, or an allergic reaction.

Before leaving the clinic, save its direct and after-hours contact routes. Ask what symptoms require sending a photo, returning the same day, or going to emergency care. A booking agent is not an appropriate medical contact.

## Flying and Scheduling

Potenza does not have one evidence-based flight-safety number that applies to every tip and setting. Its one-to-three-day recovery window makes an early-trip appointment more practical than a session immediately before an inflexible long-haul departure.

Ask the treating clinician how long it wants you nearby. If this is your first RF microneedling session, allow extra time because you do not yet know your personal swelling or pigment response.
