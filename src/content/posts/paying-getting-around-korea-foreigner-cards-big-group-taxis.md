# Paying and Getting Around Korea: Foreign Cards, Contactless & Big-Group Taxis

Korea feels cashless until the moment a perfectly ordinary foreign card fails at an unattended kiosk, or a family discovers that four people plus airport luggage do not fit safely in one standard taxi. The useful question is not whether a European fintech card is “better” than an American credit card. It is where each payment method is processed, whether the terminal supports the card's network and how much physical space the group actually needs.

This guide separates retail payments, public transport and group taxis. For a deeper comparison of prepaid travel cards and mobile-wallet behavior, keep our [Korea payment mechanics guide](/posts/korea-payment-wowpass-vs-tmoney-applepay-2026/) open as a companion.

## The 30-second setup

- Bring two physical cards on different networks or from different issuers. Keep them in separate bags.
- Enable overseas purchases and know the in-app freeze, PIN and cash-withdrawal controls before departure.
- Always choose Korean won (KRW) when a terminal offers home-currency conversion.
- Carry a small KRW reserve and a physical Korean transportation card.
- For five travelers or bulky luggage, compare a larger vehicle or two taxis before pickup time.
- Save the hotel name, address and entrance in Korean; a district name alone is not a useful taxi destination.

## Monzo and Revolut versus Chase and Amex

Monzo and Revolut are not special Korean payment rails. Their physical cards generally travel over an international network such as Mastercard or Visa, just as a Chase-issued card may. The relevant checks are the network logo, account controls, available balance, PIN status, offline-payment behavior and any issuer security decision. A UK or European card can fail, and a US card can work; nationality alone does not predict the result.

Fintech apps can be convenient because travelers can see authorizations quickly, freeze a card and manage currency balances. That does not force a Korean merchant's terminal to accept contactless payment. A merchant may accept the same physical card when inserted even if a tap fails. Some unattended machines also apply stricter domestic-card, billing-address or authentication rules than staffed counters.

Traditional US credit cards can be useful for credit limits, travel protections and points, but verify foreign transaction fees for the exact product. Chase advises travelers to understand foreign purchases and dynamic currency conversion; the practical Korea rule is to pay in KRW rather than accepting a terminal's conversion into dollars or pounds. An Amex card may work at many larger merchants, hotels and clinics, but Visa or Mastercard remains a sensible second network for smaller or unattended terminals.

There is no responsible universal “acceptance rate” for Monzo, Revolut, Chase or Amex across Korea. Merchant category, acquirer, terminal software, transaction amount, online authentication and the card issuer can all change the result. Treat any influencer percentage as anecdotal unless it identifies the tested merchants, dates and method.

## Contactless: a logo is not a guarantee

Korea has extensive card acceptance, but contactless support remains uneven by terminal. A cashier may expect insertion even when your home country defaults to tap. If Apple Pay, a contactless card or a phone wallet does nothing, do not repeat the tap indefinitely. Ask to insert the chip card, follow the terminal's PIN or signature prompt and keep a second physical card ready.

At restaurants, the employee may take the card to a counter rather than bringing a portable terminal. If you prefer to keep the card in sight, walk to the register. At self-order kiosks, switch language, look for an overseas-card option, insert the card fully and wait for the approval screen. If the kiosk fails, use a staffed register rather than attempting the same declined transaction many times.

Online Korean checkouts are a separate problem. A website can show Visa or Mastercard yet require a Korean phone number, domestic authentication plug-in or locally issued card. A card that worked in a department store can still fail online. Never assume the issuer has blocked the entire account based on one Korean checkout failure.

## Public transport is a separate wallet

Do not expect a Monzo, Revolut, Chase or Amex card to function as an open-loop tap at every subway gate or city bus. Korea Tourism Organization's 2026 transport guide directs visitors to a regular prepaid transportation card for normal subway and bus readers. Tmoney, EZL and travel products with a transit balance remain the dependable visitor setup.

The purchase channel is changing faster than the gate. Seoul announced that from March 17, 2026, 440 newer vending machines at 273 stations on Lines 1–8 accept internationally issued Visa and Mastercard cards for short-term Climate Cards and single-journey tickets, with an average service fee of about 3.7 percent. This does not mean every gate now accepts a foreign bank card directly, and it does not make every older reload machine international-card compatible.

For an ordinary Tmoney balance, keep cash available because many convenience-store and station top-up workflows still use Korean cash. Tag the same transit card when entering and leaving. Do not alternate between cards during a transfer, because the system needs one card's journey history to calculate eligible transfer discounts.

## Cash and ATM fallback without over-carrying

A modest cash reserve covers small stalls, older machines and transit reloads. Keep it separate from your cards.

At an ATM, look for a clearly marked global or international-card option. Review the Korean ATM fee and your issuer's fee before accepting. If the machine offers conversion into your home currency, decline that conversion and let the card network settle the KRW withdrawal, subject to your account's terms. A failed ATM is not proof that every Korean ATM will reject the card; try a staffed bank branch or a different network rather than repeated attempts at one machine.

## Standard taxi or larger vehicle?

A standard Korean taxi is usually appropriate for one to three travelers with moderate bags. Four legal seats do not guarantee useful luggage capacity. A group of four with four large suitcases may need two taxis or a confirmed larger vehicle. Never place luggage where it blocks a seat belt, exit or the driver's view.

Uber's Korea app can expose several licensed-taxi or premium products depending on the pickup area and current supply. Product names and availability are not a promise of passenger or baggage capacity. “Uber Black” describes a premium category, while larger products such as taxiXL, Premier Van or jumbo-taxi options may appear in certain contexts. Open the vehicle information shown for the live request and contact the assigned driver when capacity is critical.

For a family, distinguish passengers from luggage: five passengers with day bags and five passengers with airport suitcases are different bookings. If no clearly suitable larger category appears, request two standard taxis. One account may not always be the easiest way to coordinate simultaneous cars, so have a second adult ready with the destination and payment method.

## TADA for families and small groups

TADA's official web booking information says TADA Next can accommodate up to five passengers. Its current service highlights larger interior and luggage space, live calling and advance reservations. The company also advertises airport reservations from Seoul, advance fare estimates, driver and vehicle information, up to five intermediate stops and an optional child-seat selection for eligible airport bookings.

These features still require verification for the exact date, pickup and passenger mix. “Up to five passengers” does not confirm that five large suitcases also fit. Enter the full itinerary, inspect the quoted vehicle, add child-seat needs during booking and contact support if the app does not make capacity clear. For five or more vehicles or very long service, TADA directs customers to a separate custom-reservation inquiry; ordinary small-group rides use the app.

TADA is useful when a group values a roomier cabin or planned airport movement. It is not automatically cheaper than two taxis, and availability can be thinner outside its active service area or during peak demand. Compare the final estimate, cancellation terms, pickup certainty and luggage fit—not just the product name.

## Airport and hotel pickup checklist

Airport terminals and large hotels have multiple doors and legal pickup zones. Send the terminal, floor, numbered door and a recognizable meeting point. At a hotel, use the actual driveway or street-side entrance.

Before confirming a large vehicle, write down:

1. Number of adults and children.
2. Number and approximate size of suitcases, strollers and mobility devices.
3. Child-seat requirement and child's age or size.
4. Exact terminal or hotel entrance.
5. Whether the route needs intermediate stops.
6. Whether everyone can use a seat belt with the planned luggage load.

For an early flight, reserve ahead and retain a two-taxi alternative until confirmation.

## What to do when payment fails in a taxi

Confirm the selected payment method before requesting. If an in-app card fails verification, try a second card before pickup. For a street taxi, show the card logo before departure if cash is limited. Keep enough KRW for a fallback ride and request a receipt for expenses or lost-property tracing.

Do not hand over a phone containing an unlocked banking app. Show only the payment or destination screen. Split backup cards and cash between adults, and put the accommodation's Korean address on each phone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will Revolut or Monzo work in South Korea?

Their Visa or Mastercard cards can work at compatible Korean merchants, but no issuer has a universal acceptance guarantee. Bring the physical card, enable overseas use, know the PIN and keep another issuer or network available.

### Can I tap a foreign contactless card at Seoul subway gates?

Do not rely on it. Seoul's newer machines can sell certain tickets or short-term Climate Cards using international cards, but regular gates and buses still require a supported transportation card unless an explicitly marked service says otherwise.

### Is Uber Black a big-group taxi in Seoul?

Not necessarily. It is a premium product, not a universal passenger-capacity label. Check the live product details; look for an explicitly larger van, taxiXL or jumbo option, or book two taxis.

### How many people fit in TADA Next?

TADA's official English web request page states up to five passengers. Confirm luggage and child-seat capacity for the exact vehicle before travel.
