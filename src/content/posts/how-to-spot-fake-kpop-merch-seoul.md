# How to Spot Fake K-Pop Merch in Seoul: The KOMCA Hologram Check

Myeongdong and Dongdaemun street stalls sell K-pop merch alongside authorized retailers, and the difference isn't always obvious at a glance. A few checks take seconds and save you from paying official prices for a bootleg.

## Check the KOMCA hologram

Official albums carry a certification sticker from the Korea Music Copyright Association near the barcode on the back of the case. It's printed on reflective foil — tilt it and you should see a music-note pattern and KOMCA text alternate as the angle changes. A flat, single-tone silver sticker with no pattern shift is the first warning sign.

Look closer and genuine stickers also carry a finely printed serial number alongside a classification letter. If that number is smudged past legibility or simply missing, treat the item as suspect.

## Check for a label-specific seal

Major agencies — HYBE, SM, JYP, and others — add their own 3D holographic seal to official releases on top of the KOMCA sticker. Its absence on an album from a major artist is worth a second look.

## Check your receipt

Look at what business category is printed on it. A proper record retailer's receipt shows something like "record and music sales" or general retail. Street stalls and unofficial vendors often only hand over a handwritten slip, or a receipt filed under a generic goods category — neither confirms you bought an authorized release.

## Scan the barcode yourself

Official albums carry a registered global barcode (EAN/UPC). Most Korean music-chart apps (Hanteo, Circle Chart) will recognize the release instantly if you scan it. If a scan returns nothing or a mismatched title, that's a concrete signal, not just a hunch.

None of these checks are 100% definitive on their own, but together they take under a minute and catch the overwhelming majority of street bootlegs.
