## Quick Overview
Busan draws a different crowd than Seoul — more solo travelers, more first-timers landing straight into a city that doesn't hold your hand the way the capital's tourist infrastructure does. City tourism officials and local operators report a clear pattern on the ground: many visitors arrive alone, for the first time, with a Seoul-honed mental model of Korean transit that doesn't quite transfer.

That mismatch is the theme of this guide. Busan's subway ticketing works differently from Seoul's. Google Maps fails the same way it does everywhere else in Korea, but the workaround has a Busan-specific wrinkle (Gamcheon Culture Village). Cash still rules certain corners of the city even as the rest goes cashless. And solo dining has its own local rules — some dishes are built for one, while others assume a table of two before you even sit down.

## Busan Metro vs. Seoul Metro: The Differences That Actually Trip People Up

### 1. Single-Ride Tickets Are Paper QR Receipts, Not Deposit Cards
If you've used Seoul's subway, you'll instinctively look for a blue plastic token or a 500-won deposit return machine. Busan does not work that way. Single-ride tickets print as a simple paper slip with a QR code. You scan it against the optical QR sensor at the turnstile gate — same motion entering and exiting. There is no deposit to pay upfront and nothing to return afterward. Transfers between lines work normally on the same ticket.

### 2. The 1-Day Metro Pass vs. The Visit Busan Pass
- **1-Day Metro Pass (₩5,000–6,000):** Covers unlimited rides on Busan Metro Lines 1–4 for the calendar day. It pays for itself after about 4 rides. Note: automated station machines often accept **cash only in ₩1,000 bills** — use the station note-breaker if carrying ₩10,000 or ₩50,000 bills.
- **Visit Busan Pass (₩55,000 / 24h, ₩85,000 / 48h):** An all-inclusive tourist pass covering free entry to 40+ paid attractions (Lotte World Adventure Busan, Busan X the Sky, Spa Land Centum City, Skyline Luge). The physical card also works as a rechargeable transit card. If you are visiting 2+ major paid attractions in a single day, get the Visit Busan Pass; for transit-only days, stick to the 1-Day Metro Pass.

### 3. The Cash-Only T-Money Reload Trap
Foreign-issued credit cards cannot reload T-money or Cashbee cards at in-station kiosks. WOWPASS all-in-one machines (which exchange foreign cash and load usable transit/prepaid balance in one step) operate in major transport hubs like Busan Station, but coverage is thinner than Seoul. Always keep small KRW cash bills handy for reloading.

## Overcoming the Google Maps Trap
Google Maps walking and routing directions remain restricted across Korea. Switch to **Naver Map** and set the interface language to English.

### Gamcheon Culture Village Logistics
Google Maps often directs travelers to walk directly from Toseong Station. **Do not attempt this walk.** The village sits atop an extremely steep hillside.
- **The Correct Route:** Exit **Toseong Station Exit 6**, turn the corner in front of Pusan National University Hospital, and board a village minibus: route **Saha 1-1**, **Seogu 2**, or **Seogu 2-2**. All three drop off directly at the village entrance.

## Daily Friction Survival Hacks

### Street Trash Bins Are Genuinely Rare
Korea's volume-based waste-fee system removed public street bins decades ago. Dispose of trash responsibly at:
1. **Subway Station Platforms:** Bins are located near platform waiting areas, not usually at ticket gates.
2. **Convenience Stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven):** Recycle bins are located inside near counter or seating areas.
3. **Franchise Cafes:** Dedicated beverage drop shelves.
*Fines for unauthorized street littering can reach ₩100,000.*

### Public Restrooms & Passcode Locks
- **Free, clean public restrooms:** All Busan Metro stations provide clean, accessible restrooms outside or inside gates. Mega department stores (Shinsegae Centum City, Lotte Seomyeon, Lotte Gwangbok) offer pristine, free restrooms open to the public.
- **Commercial building keypads:** Cafes and restaurants often lock hallway restrooms with electronic passcodes. Always check the **bottom of your printed receipt** or the counter napkin holder for the 4-digit code.

### Cash vs. Card: The Traditional Market Exception
While metropolitan Busan is nearly 100% cashless, traditional markets like **Jagalchi Fish Market** and the street-stall alleys of **Gukje Market** often prefer cash or domestic bank transfers. Carry ₩20,000–30,000 in cash for street food and market stalls.

## Solo Dining Rules in Busan

### 1. Seomyeon Dwaeji-Gukbap (Pork Rice Soup) — 100% Solo-Friendly
Busan's premier comfort dish is meant for individual dining. Sit down and order by saying: *"Dwaeji-gukbap hana-yo"* (돼지국밥 하나요 — one pork soup, please). It arrives with rice, kimchi, salted baby shrimp, and chives to season your broth.

### 2. Haeundae K-BBQ & Seafood: Navigating the 2-Portion Minimum
Most traditional Korean BBQ and grilled seafood restaurants enforce a strict **minimum 2-portion (2인분) order**, making solo dining costly or awkward.
- **Solo Alternatives:**
  - **Milmyeon (밀면):** Busan's signature chilled wheat noodle broth, served in single bowls.
  - **Bok-guk (복국):** Clarified pufferfish soup with bean sprouts, traditionally ordered and served as single-portion sets.
