## Key Takeaways

Korea's national and public museums almost all follow the same pattern: the permanent collection is free, and only special or ticketed exhibitions cost anything — so "free" rarely means the whole building, just most of it. A handful of spots that look free on social media actually aren't: Namwon's Gwanghallu Garden charges a modest entrance fee (adults ₩4,000), and a few sights only work out free if you take a specific access route rather than the paid one. Every listing below is checked against the facility's own official page or its city/tourism-board page as of September 2026 — where the official source didn't give a clear answer, we say so instead of guessing.

## How to Read the Labels

- **Free** — general admission to the main space costs nothing.
- **Free grounds / paid extras** — the building, park, or walkway itself is free; a specific exhibition, ride, rental, or facility inside costs money.
- **Free with conditions** — free, but only through a specific access route, reservation, or eligibility (we spell out which).
- **Paid** — flagging this because it's commonly assumed to be free and isn't.
- **Unclear** — the official source doesn't state it plainly enough to promise "free" in good faith; confirm on-site or skip if budget is tight.

## Suwon (30–40 min from Seoul)

**Free** — Hwaseong Fortress's wall walk, including [Janganmun Gate](https://www.koursea.com/place/1179/), [Hwaseomun Gate](https://www.koursea.com/place/2593/), and [Banghwasuryujeong Pavilion](https://www.koursea.com/place/1236/), costs nothing to walk. It's one of the few full UNESCO World Heritage sites in the country you can experience without buying a ticket. [Suwon Museum of Art](https://www.koursea.com/place/1239/)'s Art Library and the [Hangridan-gil shopping street](https://www.koursea.com/place/1241/) are both free to browse as well.

Suwon works well as a half-day add-on before or after a Seoul itinerary — everything above is within walking distance of Suwon Station.

## Incheon (subway/AREX distance from Seoul)

**Free** — The National Hangeul Museum of the World, Incheon Art Platform's public exhibition spaces, the G-Tower 33rd-floor observation deck, Songdo Central Park's paths, the Open Port historic street and Concession Stairs, the Chinatown Romance of the Three Kingdoms mural street, and the Yongyu Sky Observatory on Yeongjong Island are all free to enter per Incheon's own tourism pages.

**Free grounds / paid extras** — Songdo Central Park charges for the boat and water-taxi rides; Incheon Art Platform runs some ticketed performances alongside its free program.

Incheon is close enough to combine two or three of these in a single day without a KTX ticket at all.

## Daegu

**Free** — [Kim Gwangseok-gil Street](https://www.koursea.com/place/540/), [Apsan Observatory](https://www.koursea.com/place/546/), [Daegu Arts Factory](https://www.koursea.com/place/570/), and Suchang Cheongchun Mansion all confirmed free.

**Free grounds / paid extras** — The National Daegu Museum's permanent collection is free; special exhibitions may not be. The Modern Culture Street (근대골목) Course 2 is a free walking route, but some of the individual museums and religious sites along the way charge separately.

## Gwangju

**Free** — [Penguin Village (양림동)](https://www.koursea.com/place/931/), [Gwangju Lake Ecology Park](https://www.koursea.com/place/1986/), [Gwangju Museum of Art](https://www.koursea.com/place/1987/), and Jeonil Building 245 (including its official guided tours) are all free.

**Free grounds / paid extras** — The Asia Culture Center's public spaces and much of its exhibition program are free, but ticketed performances and some children's programs cost extra. The National Gwangju Museum follows the standard free-permanent-collection pattern.

## Jeonju & Namwon

**Free** — In Jeonju: [Omokdae and Imokdae](https://www.koursea.com/place/1022/), [Jaman Mural Village](https://www.koursea.com/place/1026/), Palbok Art Factory, and the National Intangible Heritage Center. In Namwon: the Namwon Kim Byung-jong Museum of Art, Namwon Daum-gwan, the Manin-uichong memorial grounds, and the first stretch of the Jirisan Trail's hornbeam forest section.

**Free grounds / paid extras** — The National Jeonju Museum's general admission is free; special exhibitions may not be.

**Not free — heads up**: Gwanghallu Garden (광한루원), Namwon's signature attraction and the setting for the Chunhyang legend, charges admission — currently around ₩4,000 for adults, with reduced rates for teens and children. It's a worthwhile stop regardless, just don't budget it as a free one.

## Gyeongju

**Free** — [Gyeongju National Museum](https://www.koursea.com/place/2344/), [Daereungwon Tomb Complex](https://www.koursea.com/place/2294/), [Woljeonggyo Bridge](https://www.koursea.com/place/2322/), and the [Cheomseongdae Observatory](https://www.koursea.com/place/2304/) / Eastern Historic District are all free, and Hwangridan-gil's street itself costs nothing to walk (individual shops and cafes are, obviously, their own thing).

**Unclear**: Silla Millennium Repository — it's a genuine public storage-and-exhibition facility, but the current official page doesn't clearly spell out how much of it is open to general visitors or whether that's free. Worth checking directly before planning around it rather than assuming.

## Andong

**Free** — Wolyeonggyo Bridge and its riverside walking path.

**Free with conditions**: Buyongdae Cliff can be reached without a ticket via its own trail, but the more popular way most visitors reach the same viewpoint is through Hahoe Folk Village's paid area or a paid ferry — so whether your visit is free depends entirely on which route you take.

**Unclear**: Andong Folk Museum's outdoor exhibition area — the museum's own page doesn't clearly separate outdoor-only access from the paid indoor exhibits, so we're not listing it as a confirmed-free stop.

## Jeju

**Free** — Lee Jung-seop's former residence, the outdoor sculpture park at Jeju Museum of Art, Saebyeol Oreum, and Baegyakyi Oreum are all free (Jeju's oreum climbs generally are, aside from parking and any seasonal access closures for trail rest periods).

**Free grounds / paid extras** — The National Jeju Museum follows the standard free-permanent-collection pattern. Osulloc Tea Museum's museum and gardens are free, but the tea-tasting classes, café, and shop are not. Seopjikoji's coastal trail is free to walk; parking and a few private facilities along it are not. Sagye Beach and the Sanbangsan boardwalk are free; the Sanbanggulsa cave temple and nearby paid parking are separate.

## Before You Go

Museum hours, special exhibition schedules, and Jeju's oreum trail-closure calendar all change more often than a fixed fee ever will — confirm current hours and access on the official page linked for anything you're planning a full day trip around, especially the "unclear" entries above. If you're stacking several of these into one day, [Koursea](https://koursea.com)'s place pages for the confirmed-free spots in this guide show current ratings and map links, and our [Seoul & Busan free attractions guide](https://blog.koursea.com/posts/free-things-to-do-seoul-busan-jongno-exhibitions/) covers the two cities this piece deliberately leaves out.
