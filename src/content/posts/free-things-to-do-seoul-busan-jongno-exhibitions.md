# Free Things to Do in Seoul & Busan: Museums, Parks and Jongno Art

Free travel in Korea does not have to mean spending the day in a convenience store or walking past attractions you cannot enter. Seoul and Busan both have public museums, waterfront routes, cultural buildings and neighborhood walks that cost nothing at the entrance. The important distinction is between a free place and an entirely free day: transport, lockers, workshops, observation rides and special exhibitions may still cost extra.

This guide starts with a time-sensitive Jongno art circuit, then moves to places that work throughout the year. Always check the official notice on the day you visit. Temporary closures, ticketed special shows, crowd controls and weather restrictions can change a zero-won plan.

### A workable half-day route

Start near Anguk Station and cover the Samcheong cluster—Kukje, Gallery Hyundai and Hakgojae—before lunch. Continue west only if your group still has energy. Yéol, Artside, Art Space 3, Boan and Gallery Chosun span a wider area than the list first suggests. Galleries commonly close on Monday or between exhibitions, and free entry does not guarantee walk-in access to an opening event.

## Eight Free Things to Do in Seoul

### 1. National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관)

The permanent galleries are free, while some special exhibitions charge admission. The museum sits by Ichon Station and is large enough for several hours. Start with one floor instead of attempting every gallery. Free coat storage and lockers may be useful, but confirm size limits if you have airport luggage. The outdoor grounds are also a low-cost break with views toward Namsan.

### 2. Seoul Museum of Art, Seosomun Main Branch (서울시립미술관 서소문본관)

General admission is free except for designated special exhibitions. It is close to City Hall Station and Deoksugung-gil, making it useful in rain or extreme heat. The museum closes on Mondays and last admission precedes closing time. Check the exhibition page rather than assuming every show inside is free.

### 3. HiKR Ground (하이커 그라운드)

This Korea Tourism Organization space by Cheonggyecheon offers free digital-media and tourism experiences; separately announced programs may charge. Floors 1–4 generally operate Tuesday–Sunday, while the fifth-floor visitor center has a broader schedule. Interactive zones can be busy, so arrive near opening if filming K-pop-style content matters to you.

### 4. Cheong Wa Dae (청와대)

Admission is free, but entry is controlled. Foreign nationals may apply on site with identification, subject to the daily allocation; advance reservation is safer when available. It is closed on Tuesdays under the normal schedule. Bring your passport, allow time for screening and do not confuse free admission with guaranteed entry.

### 5. Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천)

The central stream walk is open-air and free. Enter near Cheonggye Plaza for the most accessible starting point, then walk east only as far as your schedule allows. Lower paths can close during heavy rain or high water. At night, use signed exits rather than climbing barriers to reach street level.

### 6. Hangang Parks (한강공원)

The river parks themselves are free; bicycle rental, cruises, tents, food and special events are not. Yeouido is convenient but crowded during festivals. Ttukseom, Mangwon and Ichon offer different skyline angles. Check flood closures and last subway times before settling far from an exit.

### 7. Seoul City Wall and Naksan Park (서울도성·낙산공원)

The wall trail around Naksan offers a free elevated walk and evening city views. Begin from Hyehwa for a gentler approach or from Dongdaemun for a steeper climb. Stone, stairs and winter ice make it unsuitable for every stroller or wheelchair route. Keep voices low near residential sections.

### 8. Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌한옥마을)

Walking the public lanes costs nothing, but Bukchon is a residential neighborhood rather than an outdoor theme park. Follow restricted visiting hours and quiet-zone signs, avoid doorways and skip private courtyards. For a calmer experience, use the official walking routes and visit on a weekday morning.

## Eight Free Things to Do in Busan

### 1. Museum of Contemporary Art Busan, Eulsukdo (부산현대미술관)

General admission is free except for charged special exhibitions. From Hadan Station, transfer to a bus for Eulsukdo; it is not directly outside the subway. Combine the museum with riverside ecology only in suitable weather, and check Monday closure rules.

### 2. F1963

The converted wire-factory cultural complex in Suyeong is free to enter and wander, but exhibitions, performances, cafes and programs set their own prices. Treat it as an architecture-and-bookstore stop rather than assuming every room is open. Check the current program calendar before making a long transfer.

### 3. National Maritime Museum of Korea (국립해양박물관)

Admission is free and the permanent displays suit families, but the museum is in Yeongdo and requires a bus connection from the subway. Nampo Station Exit 6 is a common transfer point for Bus 66. Monday closure and program reservations can still affect the plan.

### 4. Busan Museum of Art (부산시립미술관)

**Renovation warning:** the main building remains closed until an official reopening announcement, with a fall 2026 reopening planned. Do not travel there solely on an old “free museum” listing. Space Lee Ufan has operated separately and may have its own admission terms. Check the museum's official banner on the day.

### 5. Huinnyeoul Culture Village (흰여울문화마을)

The hillside lanes and sea views are free; cafes, galleries and transport are not. Use designated viewpoints, keep doorways clear and expect steep stairs. Travelers with mobility limitations may prefer the upper road and shorter observation segments instead of the full coastal descent.

### 6. Gamcheon Culture Village (감천문화마을)

Public lanes and viewpoints are free, while workshops and attractions may charge. This is also a living neighborhood. Buy a map only if useful, avoid photographing residents through windows and prepare for slopes. Bus connections are more practical than trying to walk uphill from the nearest subway.

### 7. Haeundae Green Railway (해운대 그린레일웨이)

Walking the coastal railway path is free. The adjacent Blue Line Park beach train and Sky Capsule are paid products, so follow Green Railway signs if your goal is a zero-won route. Start from Mipo or Cheongsapo, carry water and plan the return because the full corridor is longer than a casual photo stop.

### 8. Cheongsapo Daritdol Observatory (청사포 다릿돌전망대)

The coastal observatory is generally free, but access can close for wind, rain, maintenance or crowd control. Protective shoe covers or safety procedures may apply. Combine it with the Green Railway rather than buying a train ticket solely to reach it; verify closing time before a sunset visit.

## How to Keep a “Free” Day Actually Cheap

Cluster destinations instead of crossing the city for every saved pin. In Seoul, Jongno galleries belong together; the National Museum belongs with Ichon and the river. In Busan, Eulsukdo, Yeongdo and Haeundae are separate travel zones. A free attraction can become expensive when it creates extra taxis or a rushed meal.

Carry a charged transit card, water and a small snack. Check whether lockers, workshops, special exhibitions or reservation platforms charge separately. Save Korean names in Naver Map or Kakao Map, and take a screenshot of the final transit connection before entering a large park.

Most importantly, verify temporary exhibitions at the source. This month's Jongno list expires show by show; the evergreen section remains useful, but renovations and weather controls can still override it.
