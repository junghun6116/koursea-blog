Seoul has excellent workspaces, but “coworking” can mean a monthly membership, a credit-based lounge, a reservable one-day product, or a cafe with outlets. International visitors should confirm purchase eligibility and access method before traveling across the city.

## Key takeaways

- Do not assume every FastFive or SparkPlus branch sells a walk-in day pass.
- Confirm whether signup needs a Korean phone number, app, identity check, or local payment method.
- Published “24-hour” access may apply only to members or specific branches.
- Test video calls and VPN access before an important meeting.
- Choose a workspace near your accommodation, not the most photogenic district.

## Pick the workspace around the task

For email and writing, a quiet cafe may be enough. For confidential calls, use a bookable phone booth or meeting room. For a full workday, prioritize an ergonomic chair, reliable power, toilets, climate control, and permission to remain for several hours.

Make a requirement list: arrival time, call hours, monitor need, luggage, printing, and whether you must leave and re-enter. A cheap pass is poor value if the phone booths require separate credits or the building closes before a New York meeting.

## FastFive and FiveSpot

FastFive operates offices and its FiveSpot lounge products, but the product shown online may be a membership rather than a universal cash day pass. The official site advertises broad lounge access and selected 24-hour products; branch facilities and eligibility vary.

Confirm the exact location, product, VAT, cancellation rules, and QR or app access. A credit membership guide may describe a day unit, but that does not guarantee an anonymous tourist can walk in and pay at every reception. Ask through the official channel and keep written confirmation.

## SparkPlus lounge passes

SparkPlus publishes specific lounge-pass products. Its official information lists Metro One Day from ₩9,900 and Weekend One Day from ₩19,000, subject to location and product rules. Metro lounges inside stations may operate roughly 08:00–22:00, while some coworking-building lounges offer 24-hour member access.

One-day purchasers receive access for the selected date and branch, commonly through a QR workflow. Meeting rooms or focus desks may not be included. Prices and promotions change, so treat these as observed published starting points and verify at checkout.

## Local coworking and study cafes

Independent coworking rooms, study cafes, and hotel business lounges can be easier for one day. Search **공유오피스 일일권** for coworking day pass or **스터디카페 일일권** for a study-cafe day ticket. Study cafes are designed for quiet individual work, not calls.

Before paying, check whether the kiosk accepts a foreign card and whether entry requires SMS verification. A staffed desk is safer when you lack a Korean phone number. Hotel lounges may cost more but can solve luggage, English support, and late-call needs together.

## Reservation checklist

Ask the operator five questions in one message:

1. Can a foreign visitor buy one day without a Korean resident ID?
2. Can I pay with an overseas Visa or Mastercard?
3. Is a Korean phone number required for entry or Wi-Fi?
4. Are phone booths and meeting rooms included or separately reserved?
5. What exact hours can a one-day customer enter and re-enter?

Save the answer and branch address. “24 hours” on a brand page can refer to another membership tier. Building elevators and reception desks may also have separate hours.

## Wi-Fi speed and reliability

Do not choose solely from an advertised speed. Shared Wi-Fi performance varies with occupancy, access point, VPN, and device. On arrival, run a brief speed and latency test, join the video platform, and test your microphone. Keep mobile data as backup.

For confidential work, use your company VPN and avoid leaving screens or papers visible. Ask whether there is a separate guest network. Public networks should not be treated as trusted simply because the office looks premium.

## Calls across time zones

Seoul is 13 or 14 hours ahead of New York depending on US daylight saving. A New York morning call often falls late in Seoul. Confirm building and phone-booth access during the actual call window, not only when reception is staffed.

Use headphones and book an enclosed space. Open lounges are unsuitable for client data or repeated calls. If booths cannot be reserved, arrive early and identify a backup lobby or hotel workspace.

## Power, monitors, and ergonomics

Korea uses 220V Type C/F sockets. Most laptop chargers support 100–240V, but check the label and bring a stable adapter. Do not balance a heavy universal adapter loosely in a wall outlet.

Ask whether external monitors, HDMI cables, lockers, and chairs are first-come-first-served. Photograph the desk condition before using borrowed equipment. Keep valuables with you during breaks unless a staffed locker is included.

## Printing and scanning

Many coworking products advertise multifunction printers, but usage can require member credentials or paid credits. Ask before relying on it for an immigration, business, or medical document. Keep files in PDF and carry a USB drive plus appropriate to your security policy.

For alternatives outside coworking offices, use the companion [Seoul printing and copying guide](/posts/seoul-printing-document-copying-guide/).

## A practical one-day workflow

Reserve the day before, screenshot confirmation and Korean address, and arrive 20 minutes before work. Test access, Wi-Fi, power, calls, toilets, and break policy immediately. Download critical files locally and enable mobile hotspot backup.

Take lunch outside peak time or bring a low-odor meal only if allowed. Before leaving, log out of shared devices, clear printer queues, collect cables, and check the re-entry rule.

## When a cafe is better

A cafe is better for two hours of low-stakes work near sightseeing. Buy appropriately, avoid occupying a large table during a rush, and obey laptop restrictions. A coworking day pass is better when missing a meeting would cost more than the desk.

## What “24 hours” really means

A building can be open around the clock while the reception desk, day-pass product, elevators, or air-conditioning follows shorter hours. Some access systems work only for monthly members after the staffed period. Ask whether your exact pass permits entry and re-entry at the time of your overseas call, not whether the brand advertises 24-hour service in general.

Late-night workers should verify the building exit process, toilet access, neighborhood transport, and whether phone calls are permitted. Save the Korean address and a screenshot of access instructions before reception closes. If the pass depends on a QR code, confirm that it remains visible without mobile data.

## Payment, identity, and phone verification

Registration may require an app, domestic phone authentication, local payment method, passport, or advance approval. These requirements differ by product and branch. A foreign card accepted by the company’s regular billing system may still fail on a Korean mobile checkout.

Contact the operator before paying for transport across Seoul. Ask four direct questions: Can a tourist without Korean residency buy this product? Can they pay with an overseas card? Is Korean SMS required? Will staff be present at first entry? Keep the written reply.

Never send a full passport image through an unofficial social account. Use the operator’s stated channel, mask unnecessary details where permitted, and understand why identification is requested before submitting it.

## Choosing a neighborhood

Gangnam and Yeoksam work for business meetings but create a long commute if your accommodation is north of the river. Seoul Station and Gongdeok simplify airport movement. Hongdae is convenient for nightlife and western Seoul, while Euljiro and Jongno balance central sightseeing with transport.

Choose the workspace closest to the hardest commitment, not the trendiest district. A dependable desk five minutes from a client meeting is more valuable than a photogenic lounge requiring two transfers. Check the final walking route because a “station-adjacent” building may connect to a distant exit.

## Security checklist for remote workers

Use a trusted VPN when required by your organization, enable device encryption, and avoid discussing confidential details in an open lounge. Lock the screen every time you leave the desk. Do not connect unknown USB accessories or save credentials on shared equipment.

For regulated or sensitive work, confirm whether the operator provides a private room and what acoustic privacy it actually offers. Glass booths reduce noise but do not guarantee confidentiality. Headphones protect others from your call, not your spoken information from them.

Before departure, remove documents from printers, disconnect casting sessions, forget networks if appropriate, and check every outlet for chargers. Report lost access cards immediately so the operator can disable them.

## Backup plan when access fails

Keep one nearby staffed cafe, hotel business center, or second coworking product in reserve. Store presentation files locally and in an approved cloud location. Carry hotspot data, a charging cable, plug adapter, and compact battery.

If a critical meeting begins within an hour, prioritize stable audio over an ideal background. Join early from the backup location, tell participants if video must remain off, and resolve the workspace problem afterward. Seoul has abundant desks, but the exact branch you selected can still fail.
