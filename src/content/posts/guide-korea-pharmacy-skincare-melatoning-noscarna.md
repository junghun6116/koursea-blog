## Olive Young Is Cosmetics. The Pharmacy Next Door Sells Medicine.
Every foreign visitor's K-beauty haul starts at Olive Young, and it should — but Olive Young sells cosmetics: serums, essences, sheet masks, formulated to be gentle enough for daily use without medical oversight. A Korean *yakguk* (약국, pharmacy) sells something categorically different: registered over-the-counter drugs (일반의약품) with active pharmaceutical ingredients, approved by Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (식약처/MFDS) for specific medical indications — pigmentation, scarring, active acne — the same category of treatment a dermatologist might reach for, just sold without a prescription.

That's also why the price gap is so wide. A tube that would cost significantly more as a prescription dermatology treatment in the US or Europe runs roughly $10–25 in a Korean pharmacy, largely because Korea's OTC drug-pricing structure keeps these ingredients affordable and pharmacist-accessible rather than gatekept behind a specialist visit. You don't need Korean insurance or a doctor's note — you need to walk in and ask for it by name.

## The 4 Hero Products, and What They Actually Treat
These four are genuinely different tools for genuinely different skin problems — the mistake most first-time buyers make is treating them as interchangeable "acne cream." They're not. Here's what each one is actually approved for, sourced from each product's official MFDS-registered indication.

### Melatoning Cream — Pigmentation, Melasma & Sunspots
- **Active ingredient:** Hydroquinone 2% (20mg/g).
- **Classification:** OTC, no prescription required.
This is Korea's most widely available hydroquinone product, approved for melasma (기미), freckles, age spots, and other excess-melanin pigmentation. The official label instructs applying twice daily, morning and before bed — but many dermatologists recommend applying it only at night in practice, since hydroquinone increases photosensitivity and daytime use raises the risk of the exact sun-triggered pigmentation you're trying to treat. Whichever schedule you follow, **daily sunscreen is not optional** while using this product — skipping it can make pigmentation worse, not better.

If you're pairing this with an in-clinic pico-toning laser session, ask your clinic how to sequence the two — Melatoning is commonly used to prep or maintain pigmentation between laser sessions, but timing around the treatment's redness/downtime window matters and is worth confirming with whoever performs the procedure. Compare that clinical pathway with our [private-clinic Pico Laser planning guide](/beauty/laser-toning-pico-private-clinic/) before booking.

### Noscarna Gel — Scar Texture (Read the Fine Print)
- **Active ingredients:** Heparin sodium 500IU, Allantoin 50mg, Dexpanthenol 100mg (per gram).
- **Classification:** OTC, no prescription required.
Noscarna is approved as a post-wound-healing treatment for hypertrophic and keloid-type scars, acne scars, and surgical scars — it's a scar-texture and healing-support gel, not an active-breakout treatment, so don't reach for it while you still have inflamed acne (pair it with Acnon or use Noscarna afterward once a spot has healed, not during). One honest caveat worth knowing before you buy: Korean pharmaceutical trade press has reported an ongoing dispute among Korea's Central Pharmaceutical Affairs Committee over whether Noscarna's approved "acne scar" indication was adequately supported by clinical evidence at the time of approval. It remains a legally sold, officially indicated OTC product as of this writing — but if scar-texture improvement is your main goal, treat "acne scars" as the most debated of its several approved uses rather than its best-proven one. For a separate in-clinic skin-rejuvenation option, review the dosage and pricing caveats in our [Rejuran Healer private-clinic guide](/beauty/rejuran-healer-private-clinic/).

### Acnon Cream — Calming Active, Inflamed Breakouts
- **Active ingredients:** Ibuprofen piconol 30mg, Isopropylmethylphenol 10mg (per gram).
- **Classification:** OTC, no prescription required.
Acnon's approved indications are red, inflamed, pus-filled breakouts (화농성여드름) and general pimples — ibuprofen piconol is a topical anti-inflammatory, so this is the one to reach for on an angry, actively-inflamed spot for fast calming, not a long-term scar or pigmentation fix. It's not indicated specifically for severe cystic acne, so if you're dealing with large, deep, painful nodules regularly, that's a dermatologist visit, not a pharmacy-counter fix.

### Acrin Gel — Blackheads & Clogged Pores
- **Active ingredient:** Salicylic acid 2% (20mg/g).
- **Classification:** OTC, no prescription required.
A BHA exfoliant gel for general acne and clogged, comedonal pores — the mechanism (salicylic acid dissolving the debris inside a pore) is what makes it useful for blackheads and whiteheads specifically, versus the inflammatory red bumps Acnon targets. The label recommends starting with once-daily application because it can dry out skin faster than expected; if you notice peeling, drop to every other day rather than pushing through it.

## How to Actually Buy These at a Korean Pharmacy
Korean pharmacists in tourist-dense neighborhoods are used to foreign customers pointing at their phone screen, and that's the easiest approach — pull up a photo of the product name (Korean or English) and show it rather than attempting pronunciation. If you want the phrase anyway:

> **"이거 있어요?"** (*i-geo iss-eo-yo?*) — "Do you have this?" — said while showing the name or a photo of the packaging.

None of the four products above require a *처방전* (prescription) — they're all classified 일반의약품 (general OTC medication), meaning you can walk up to the counter and ask for them directly, the same way you'd buy any over-the-counter product. If a pharmacist asks a few questions about your skin or how you plan to use it, that's normal pharmacist practice, not a sign you need paperwork you don't have.

## Duty-Free Shopping: What's Actually Eligible in 2026
Worth being precise here, because 2026 changed one part of Korea's tax-refund landscape and left another part untouched. As of January 1, 2026, Korea ended VAT-refund eligibility specifically for **aesthetic clinic procedures** (Botox, filler, laser treatments and similar). That change applies to medical *services*, not retail *goods* — pharmacy purchases of physical OTC products remain covered under Korea's standard tourist tax-refund system for goods, completely unaffected by the procedure-refund change.

In practice: not every pharmacy is registered for this. Registration as a 사후면세점 (tax-free affiliated store) is opt-in, not automatic, so look for a "Tax Free" or 사후면세점 sticker in the window — pharmacies in tourist-heavy areas like Myeongdong, Hongdae, Gangnam Station, and Busan's Seomyeon are the most likely to be registered. When you check out at a registered pharmacy, hand over your passport and say "tax free, please" — the clerk processes the deduction on the spot (immediate/즉시환급) rather than making you claim it later at the airport. Key thresholds:
- Minimum single purchase: **₩15,000**
- In-store immediate refund limit: up to **₩1,000,000** per transaction
- Cumulative cap per trip: **₩5,000,000**

## A Quick Word on Skin Sensitivity
Everything above is a real, MFDS-registered active pharmaceutical ingredient, not a cosmetic formula — which means individual reactions vary more than they would with a standard moisturizer. Patch-test any new product on a small area of skin (inner forearm or behind the ear) for 24–48 hours before applying it to your face, especially the hydroquinone and salicylic acid products, and stop use if you see persistent redness, burning, or irritation. If you're pregnant, breastfeeding, or on other topical prescription treatments, ask the pharmacist directly before buying.
