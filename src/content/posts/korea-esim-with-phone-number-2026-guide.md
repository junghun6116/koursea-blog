# Korean eSIM Guide (2026): Why Data-Only Fails for CatchTable, Delivery & Queues

Most Korea eSIM advice online boils down to one piece of advice: buy the cheapest unlimited-data eSIM you can find before you land. It's not wrong, exactly — but it skips the one detail that ends up mattering most for anyone trying to actually use Korea like a local: whether that eSIM comes with a phone number that can **receive a text message**.

A huge share of the apps you'll want on this trip — CatchTable for restaurant bookings, Baemin for delivery, Kakao T for taxis — gate access behind SMS-based identity verification, and a pure data-only eSIM simply can't clear that step.

This guide covers why that happens, which carrier and SIM/eSIM products actually solve it, the real limits of Korea's identity-verification system for foreigners, and the device compatibility issues (especially for US-bought phones) that can derail your plan before you even land.

## Why "Just Get Unlimited Data" Backfires

Korea's app ecosystem — Baemin (delivery), Kakao T (taxis), and to a lesser extent CatchTable — relies on **본인인증** (phone-based identity verification): you get a text with a code, enter it, and the app confirms you're a real, verifiable phone line.

A data-only eSIM generally either has no phone number attached at all, or has one that cannot send or receive SMS. LG U+'s official tourist eSIM documentation states this plainly: *"If you use Data-only services, SMS verification is not available."* If the app you're trying to open requires that code, a data-only eSIM stops you cold — no workaround, no retry.

### The CatchTable Exception

CatchTable is a partial exception worth knowing about. The foreigner-facing **CatchTable Global** app lets you sign up with Google, Apple, or email — no Korean phone number required. The **domestic** CatchTable app, the one Korean locals use, still requires telecom-based verification. If you've downloaded the wrong version, that's often the actual problem, not your SIM.

## Which Carrier/eSIM Actually Gives You a Working 010 Number

This is where it gets genuinely confusing, because "eSIM" isn't one product — it's a category, and only some options in it include real SMS/voice capability.

- **Standard tourist data-only eSIMs:** The cheap, popular ones sold on general travel marketplaces provide data only, with no working number by design.
- **Data + Voice plans from major carriers:** LG U+, for example, sells a separate Data+Voice tourist SIM (commonly picked up in person at the airport rather than activated remotely) that includes a functioning number.
- **SKT-network resellers marketing a real 010 number with voice/SMS:** These exist and are commonly used, but read the fine print: some require an extra registration step after you land, and the shortest (1-day) plans are often still data-only even when longer plans on the same product line aren't.
- **알뜰폰 (MVNO) short-term tourist SIMs:** Several are explicitly marketed as "010번호 발신·수신가능" (010 number, send and receive capable) and are worth considering if a specific product's tourist eligibility is confirmed before you buy.

> **Practical rule:** Don't trust the word "unlimited data" alone — look specifically for "Data + Voice" or an explicit SMS/문자 수신 (SMS reception) claim in the product description. If it's not there, assume it's data-only.

## The Identity-Verification Wall Foreigners Actually Hit

Even with a working 010 number, there's a second layer to understand: Korea's telecom-based verification system is built around a number being registered to a real identity in a carrier's database — not just the ability to receive a text.

This domestic verification architecture is built for a market where every number traces back to a resident ID. A short-term tourist SIM can receive an SMS just fine and still occasionally hit friction on banking or payment services that expect a deeper registration match, though this varies by app and isn't a universal blocker for restaurant waits or taxi hailings.

For travelers staying long-term who want frictionless access to every Korean financial app, registering an Alien Registration Card (ARC) after arrival and getting a postpaid line in person remains the standard route.

## Physical SIM Pickup vs. eSIM: The Real Tradeoffs

- **Physical SIM at Incheon Airport:** Counters for the three major carriers operate in both terminals, with several open 24 hours. Staff activate the card on the spot, ensuring confirmed connectivity before you leave the airport.
- **eSIM activated before landing:** No counter visit, no queues, and you can be online the moment you touch down. However, activation is self-service, meaning you troubleshoot independently if an install profile fails.

## Device Compatibility: The Trap Before You Fly

Two device issues frequently catch travelers off guard:

1. **Carrier-locked iPhones:** If your iPhone was purchased through a US carrier (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) and still has an active carrier lock, it will refuse to install an eSIM profile from a different provider. Check under **Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock** before you fly.
2. **US-market Samsung Galaxy phones:** Certain Galaxy models sold specifically through US carriers have shipped without eSIM support or with locked eSIM managers. Confirm your exact model variant supports dual eSIM before assuming compatibility.
