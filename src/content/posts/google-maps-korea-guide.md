Google Maps is excellent for saving restaurants, checking reviews, and understanding the shape of a city. In South Korea, however, it is not always the most reliable tool for turn-by-turn walking or driving directions. A route that looks simple may return no walking option, send you to an inconvenient entrance, or omit the subway exit that matters most.

The practical solution is not to delete Google Maps. Use it as one part of a three-app toolkit: **Google Maps for discovery and saved lists, Naver Map for detailed local navigation, and KakaoMap as a strong Korean alternative**. This Google Maps Korea guide explains exactly how to build that workflow, including how to set up Naver Map English labels and search successfully when a venue has several names.

## The short answer

For most visitors, the best setup is:

1. Save interesting places in Google Maps before the trip.
2. Install Naver Map and KakaoMap while you still have reliable Wi-Fi.
3. Copy the Korean place name or road address into Naver Map for the final route.
4. Check the subway exit number before leaving the platform.
5. Keep the destination in Korean for taxi drivers and local staff.

Google notes that navigation features and lane information are not available everywhere. In Korea, those limitations can be more noticeable than travelers expect, particularly for walking and driving. App coverage and interfaces can change, so confirm important airport transfers and late-night routes shortly before travel.

## Why Google Maps works differently in South Korea

The important distinction is between **finding a place** and **navigating to it**. Google Maps can display a Korean venue, show user photos, and let you save it. The same listing may still lack a useful pedestrian route or precise entrance guidance.

Travelers often experience three problems:

- A walking route is unavailable or less detailed than expected.
- Romanized venue names do not match the current Korean listing.
- A large complex has multiple entrances, but the route ends on the wrong side.

This matters in neighborhoods such as Seongsu, Euljiro, Hongdae, and Ikseon-dong, where a destination may sit inside an alley, on an upper floor, or behind a building with a different street-facing name. It also matters at huge underground stations. “Gangnam Station” is not a complete destination when exits can place you several busy intersections apart.

## Step 1: Install your Korea navigation toolkit before arrival

Download Naver Map and KakaoMap before boarding your flight. Open each app once, grant only the permissions you are comfortable sharing, and confirm that location access works. If the app offers an interface-language setting, select English; menu names can move after updates, so use the app's settings search when necessary.

Save the web versions too:

- [Naver Map](https://map.naver.com/)
- [KakaoMap](https://map.kakao.com/)

The web links are useful if an app deep link fails, you are borrowing a device, or you want to send a destination through a messenger. Keeping both apps is not excessive: one can rescue you when a listing is missing, a spelling differs, or a temporary network error affects the other.

## Step 2: Search with the Korean name first

The most dependable query is usually the official Korean venue name. Search engines may treat “Myeongdong Kyoja,” “Myeong-dong Gyoja,” and “명동교자” as different strings even though a traveler means the same restaurant.

Use this order:

1. Official Korean business name, such as `명동교자 본점`.
2. Korean road address, such as `서울 중구 명동10길 29`.
3. English name plus neighborhood.
4. A landmark or subway station near the destination.

Copy the Korean text from the venue's official website, hotel message, booking confirmation, or Koursea place card. Then verify the listing by comparing photos, category, district, and phone number. Do not select the first similarly named branch without checking the address.

## Step 3: Read subway exits as part of the address

In Seoul, an exit number is often as important as the station name. A correct route to the wrong exit can add ten minutes, stairs, and a difficult road crossing.

Before the train arrives, record:

- Line name and color
- Direction or terminal station
- Transfer station
- Final exit number
- Landmark after leaving the exit

Naver Map typically provides more locally useful exit and transfer detail than a visitor expects from a global map. Still, look at station signs because construction can temporarily close an exit or elevator. Travelers with luggage should search for the elevator symbol instead of assuming the nearest exit is step-free.

For a complex trip, take a screenshot showing the final two stations and exit. A screenshot remains useful in a tunnel even if mobile data drops.

## Step 4: Build routes around entrances, not map pins

A map pin can identify the correct building while still leading you to the wrong entrance. Department stores, underground malls, palaces, stadiums, and office towers commonly have entrance-specific access rules.

For COEX, for example, navigate first to the correct subway exit or named gate, then search for the shop inside the complex. For a concert venue, save both the arena and your planned post-show meeting point. For a palace, confirm the visitor gate rather than walking toward the geometric center of the grounds.

When a listing includes a floor number, save it. Korean addresses often identify the building but not the tenant's floor in the largest text. A note such as `3층` means third floor; `지하 1층` or `B1` means the first basement level.

## Step 5: Prepare a taxi-ready destination card

Taxi navigation works best when the driver can read the Korean name and road address. Keep a large-text note in this format:

> **여기로 가 주세요.**  
> Please take me here.
>
> **명동교자 본점**  
> **서울 중구 명동10길 29**

Show the address before the car starts moving and confirm the general district. If you use Uber in Korea or Kakao T, compare the app's destination pin with the Korean address. A translated hotel name alone can point to the wrong branch.

For the return journey, save your accommodation's Korean address at check-in. Do not wait until late at night, when your battery is low, to search for it.

## A realistic Seoul example: Hongdae to Seongsu

Suppose you discover a Seongsu café through Google reviews while staying in Hongdae. Save the café in Google Maps, then copy its Korean name into Naver Map. Select public transport and compare the proposed transfers. Check whether the route uses Seongsu Station, Seoul Forest Station, or Ttukseom Station; these are useful for different parts of the neighborhood.

Before leaving the final train, check the exit and walk time. If the café is in a converted factory building, compare the storefront photo with the street view or user photos. On the way back, run the route again instead of reversing your memory: late-evening train intervals and the best transfer can differ.

This workflow takes less than a minute and is more reliable than trusting one English pin from discovery through arrival.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Searching only the English translation

Automatic translations can be unofficial, outdated, or shared by multiple businesses. Keep Korean text beside the English name.

### Ignoring branch names

Words such as `본점` (main branch), `강남점` (Gangnam branch), and `성수점` (Seongsu branch) change the destination. Confirm the branch before opening directions.

### Treating estimated walking time as exact

Add time for station corridors, elevators, crowds, traffic lights, hills, and finding an upper-floor entrance. A five-minute map estimate can become fifteen minutes with luggage.

### Relying on live data underground

Subway tunnels and packed concert areas can have inconsistent connectivity. Screenshot the essential route and meeting point.

### Trusting opening hours without a second check

Holiday hours, break times, last orders, and pop-up schedules change frequently. Verify critical visits through an official channel on the same day.

## Useful Korean navigation phrases

Save these phrases as a show card:

- **이 주소로 가 주세요.** — Please take me to this address.
- **이 출구에 엘리베이터가 있나요?** — Is there an elevator at this exit?
- **여기가 맞나요?** — Is this the correct place?
- **가장 가까운 지하철역이 어디예요?** — Where is the nearest subway station?
- **길을 잃었어요. 도와주세요.** — I am lost. Please help me.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Google Maps work at all in Korea?

Yes. It remains useful for reviews, photos, broad transit planning, saved lists, and sharing. The weakness is expecting every walking or driving feature to behave exactly as it does in countries where Google has fuller navigation coverage.

### Is Naver Map available in English?

Naver Map provides English support for many interface elements and place labels, though some local listings and user content remain Korean. Searching the Korean name is still the most reliable habit.

### Should I use Naver Map or KakaoMap?

Start with Naver Map if you want an English-friendly local navigation workflow. Keep KakaoMap as a backup and comparison tool. The better result can vary by venue and neighborhood.

### Can I navigate without a Korean phone number?

Basic map search and route planning generally do not require a Korean phone number. Some reservations, queues, or account features may have separate verification rules.

### What should I save offline?

Save your hotel name and address in Korean, airport transfer route, final subway exit, emergency contacts, and any time-sensitive venue address. Screenshots are often more dependable than expecting a full map to remain available offline.

## Final checklist

Before your first day in Korea, install two local map options, test location access, and save your accommodation in Korean. For every important stop, confirm the branch, floor, nearest exit, and same-day hours. Use Google Maps to discover; use a Korean mapping service to finish the journey.

That small change turns “Google Maps does not work in Korea” from a travel crisis into a manageable app choice. The goal is not loyalty to one platform. It is arriving at the correct entrance with enough battery, data, and Korean text to recover when plans change.
