# Andong Hahoe Village Day Trip: How to Plan the Visit from Andong Station

Hahoe Folk Village is a rewarding Andong day trip if you plan the local connection before you arrive. It is not a station-side attraction: first reach Andong, then check the current bus or other local transport to the village and leave enough time for the return. The village itself is best explored slowly, with its lanes, traditional homes, river setting, and cultural sites treated as a lived-in place rather than a film set.

## Getting to Andong and onward to Hahoe

Start by searching Korail for Andong Station on your travel date. The official tourism listing identifies Andong Station and Andong Bus Terminal as arrival points for visitors traveling onward to Hahoe. Andong's bus information system offers route and stop searches, while the city's timetable page posts service updates. Search for “Hahoe Village” or “하회마을” shortly before travel; route numbers, departures, and seasonal services may change.

VISITKOREA has published a route description from Andong's bus terminal to the village, but treat that as a planning reference—not a promise of today's departure pattern. Check the live Andong bus system and the village's own notice for the return trip as carefully as the outbound journey. If a bus timetable does not fit your train, compare a taxi or other locally available option before committing to a tight same-day schedule.

## What to see inside the village

The village's setting is part of the experience: the Nakdong River bends around the settlement, and historic houses and courtyards sit within a landscape that feels different from a purpose-built museum complex. Andong's official tourism site identifies the village with the Pungsan Ryu clan and points visitors to the preserved houses and Hahoe mask-dance culture. VISITKOREA also lists the Hahoe International Mask Museum and village heritage stops.

Use the official map and visitor guidance to plan a loop instead of trying to see every lane in a rush. Some homes and courtyards are private or have their own access rules. Stay on visitor routes, respect residents' privacy, and follow signs around homes and cultural properties.

## Time your visit around the mask-dance tradition

Hahoe Byeolsingut Talnori is closely associated with the village, but performance schedules are not something to guess from an old travel post. Check the official village notice for the date you plan to visit; performance days, start times, and arrangements can vary. If the performance is central to your trip, confirm it before buying a long-distance ticket or arranging your local transfer.

## A practical day-trip shape

- Travel to Andong Station or Andong Bus Terminal and check the onward route before leaving the arrival point.
- Visit the village at a pace that allows time for its lanes, cultural sites, and the setting around the Nakdong River.
- If a mask-dance performance is scheduled, build the visit around the official timetable rather than assuming it runs daily.
- Check the last practical return bus or other connection before settling into the afternoon.
- If you have extra time in Andong, plan city sights and meals separately; Koursea's [Andong restaurant guide](https://blog.koursea.com/posts/best-restaurants-andong-jjimdak-salted-mackerel/) covers local dishes and dining areas.

## Before you go

Save the village's official address and Andong's current bus-information page offline or in a note. Check both the outbound and return journeys, especially on weekends, public holidays, or dates when Andong announces seasonal transport. If anything is unclear, ask the Andong tourist information center or the village visitor office instead of relying on an old timetable screenshot.
