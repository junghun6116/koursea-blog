# Kakao T or Uber Payment Declined in Korea? What Actually Fixes It

Your ride shows up, you get in, and then the app throws a payment error instead of confirming the trip. This is one of the most common friction points for visitors using Kakao T or Uber in Korea, and it's a card-network issue, not something wrong with your account or your bank balance.

## Why it happens

Kakao T's default in-app payment flow is built around domestic Korean card rails. Foreign-issued cards — even mainstream Visa and Mastercard — authorize inconsistently depending on the issuing bank and how the transaction is coded. You can have plenty of available credit and still get declined.

## The fix: switch to Pay to Driver

Inside the Kakao T app, there's a payment method option to **pay the driver directly** instead of charging the card on file automatically. This routes payment to a card reader in the vehicle or to cash, sidestepping whatever is causing the in-app authorization to fail. Set this before you book, or switch it if a ride keeps failing to confirm.

## If you don't have the language to explain the problem

Keep this ready to show the driver on your phone screen if a payment method doesn't work as expected: **"카드 결제가 안 됩니다. 어떻게 하면 될까요?"** ("My card payment isn't going through — how should I pay?"). Drivers deal with this regularly and will point you to whatever payment method actually works in their vehicle.

## Uber runs into the same wall

Uber's Korea service is largely built on partnered taxi fleets rather than independent drivers, so it shares much of the same in-app payment infrastructure — and the same failure pattern. If in-app payment won't go through, the driver can typically take payment directly once you arrive, the same way as a Kakao T ride.
