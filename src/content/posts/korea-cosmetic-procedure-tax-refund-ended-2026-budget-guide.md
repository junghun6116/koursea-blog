Foreign visitors planning Botox, Rejuran, Potenza, laser toning, fillers or lifting treatments in Seoul need to update their budgets. Korea's special VAT refund program for qualifying cosmetic medical procedures ended on **December 31, 2025**. Procedures supplied on or after **January 1, 2026** no longer qualify for the former medical-procedure refund.

This does not mean every Korean tourist tax-refund program has disappeared. Product purchases at participating tax-refund stores remain under a separate retail-goods system. The practical rule for a 2026 clinic visit is simple:

> Budget using the clinic's final VAT-inclusive checkout price. Do not subtract an expected airport refund from your treatment cost.

This guide explains what ended, what remains and how to compare Seoul clinic quotes without being misled by package wording, omitted tax, product quantities or unrealistic downtime assumptions.

## What changed on January 1, 2026?

Until the end of 2025, eligible foreign tourists could receive a refund of VAT paid on qualifying cosmetic medical services at registered medical institutions. That treatment-specific program reached its statutory end on December 31, 2025. The National Assembly Budget Office describes the policy change as the termination of the foreign-tourist cosmetic medical service VAT refund at the end of 2025.

For procedures performed from January 1, 2026:

- Do not assume cosmetic treatment VAT can be reclaimed at the airport.
- Do not select a clinic based on an old page promising a 7–10% medical refund.
- Do not count an outdated “tax refund available” badge as part of your budget.
- Ask the clinic to state the complete amount payable before treatment.
- Keep a written quotation showing the treatment, quantity, brand, device or tip count, anesthesia, consumables and aftercare.

Some clinic pages and older travel articles may still display pre-2026 information. Ask for written confirmation if a page continues to advertise a medical-procedure refund.

### The Korean price phrase to show

> **이 가격에 부가세 포함인가요?**  
> *I gagyeoge bugase pohamingayo?*  
> **Is VAT included in this price?**

For a complete quote, use:

> **추가 결제 비용까지 포함한 최종 금액을 적어 주세요.**  
> *Please write down the final price, including any additional charges.*

A written total is more useful than a verbal estimate, especially when clinics structure their packages differently.

## Clinic procedures and Olive Young shopping are separate

The end of the cosmetic-procedure refund does **not** end Korea's tourist refund system for eligible physical goods. At participating retail stores displaying an authorized Tax Refund or Tax Free sign, eligible tourists may still receive an immediate or later refund for qualifying merchandise. That may include cosmetics or skincare bought at a participating Olive Young branch, but eligibility is determined by the store, product, purchase amount and traveler—not by the brand name alone.

Korea Tourism Organization guidance says an immediate retail refund generally requires an eligible purchase of at least **15,000 KRW** and less than **1,000,000 KRW** in one transaction, with no more than **5,000,000 KRW** in immediate-refund purchases during the trip. Passport verification is required, and the goods must meet the export conditions.

| Expense | 2026 treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Botox, filler, laser, Rejuran or another cosmetic medical procedure | Former foreign-tourist medical VAT refund ended |
| Cosmetics or skincare at an eligible tax-refund shop | Retail-goods refund may remain available |
| Olive Young purchase | Depends on the branch, product, transaction amount and tourist eligibility |
| Prescription medicine or clinic aftercare service | Do not assume retail refund eligibility |
| Airport claim for a 2026 cosmetic procedure | Do not budget for it |

Keep clinic treatment costs and retail shopping costs in separate budget categories.

## A realistic clinic budget for 2026

Start with the final clinic price, then account for expenses travelers often miss:

1. Consultation or interpretation fees
2. Anesthesia or numbing cream
3. Device tips, cartridges, needles or consumables
4. Prescribed medicine and pharmacy aftercare
5. Follow-up visits
6. Transport back to the hotel
7. Recovery supplies such as sunscreen or hydrocolloid patches
8. A contingency allowance for plan changes

A practical contingency is **10–15% above the written clinic quote**. This is not an estimated refund or tax calculation. It is a travel-planning buffer for optional additions, medicine, transportation and aftercare.

If a clinic advertises Potenza at 290,000 KRW, ask whether that means one zone or the full face, which tip is included, whether numbing cream costs extra, whether a skin booster is optional, who performs the procedure and whether follow-up care is included. A 290,000 KRW headline price and a 350,000 KRW all-inclusive quote may represent nearly the same service once extras are itemized.

## Factory-style versus private clinics

Neither model is automatically better. The meaningful differences are consultation time, doctor continuity, waiting time, language support and how packages are framed.

| Comparison point | Factory-style clinic | Private / solo-doctor clinic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical pricing | Lower and promotion-driven | Higher and consultation-driven |
| Daily patient volume | High | Lower |
| Practitioner continuity | May vary by visit | More likely to remain consistent |
| Consultation depth | Often brief and standardized | Usually more individualized |
| Waiting time | Can increase during promotions | More appointment-oriented |
| English support | Functional at many tourist-facing branches | Often better for nuanced discussions |
| Best suited to | Standardized repeat or maintenance treatment | First visits, complex concerns or placement-sensitive work |
| Main pricing risk | Add-ons and abbreviated consultation | Premium package framing and larger quoted quantities |

## Compare units instead of package names

“Full face,” “premium,” “signature” and “foreigner package” are not standardized medical quantities. For fair comparisons, request:

- Botox brand and number of units
- Filler brand and milliliters
- Rejuran syringe count and total cc
- Ultherapy or Shurink shot count
- Potenza tip type and treated area
- Whether medication, anesthesia and follow-up are included

For injectables, ask to see the product name and unopened packaging before treatment. For energy-based devices, confirm whether the quote includes the advertised shot count or tip.

## Downtime belongs in the budget

A treatment can disrupt a trip even when it does not require formal medical leave. Potenza redness commonly lasts one to three days. Rejuran injection bumps may remain visible for 24–48 hours. Laser toning redness may settle within hours, while pigment-focused treatments can produce later flaking. Botox usually has no mandatory downtime, but the clinic may give positioning and exercise precautions. Filler bruising or swelling can take several days and sometimes one to two weeks to settle.

Do not schedule an unfamiliar procedure immediately before a wedding, concert, long-haul flight or important photography session. Leave a recovery buffer and follow the treating clinician's instructions.

Seek prompt medical attention for severe or worsening pain, breathing difficulty, visual disturbance, spreading redness, fever or other unexpected symptoms. Call **119** for a medical emergency in Korea.

## Card payments and cash-discount warnings

A Korean credit-card merchant may not pass its merchant processing fee to the cardholder as a separate surcharge. If a clinic says the price increases solely because you are paying by card, ask for a written breakdown.

> **카드 결제 수수료가 별도로 붙나요?**  
> *Is there a separate card-payment surcharge?*

A genuine cash promotion and an unlawful card-fee transfer are not necessarily the same thing, so ask calmly for the advertised price, final card price, reason for any difference and an itemized receipt. Paying by card also creates a clearer transaction record if you later need to question a charge.

## The bottom line

From January 1, 2026, foreign visitors should treat the displayed final clinic charge as the real treatment cost. The former cosmetic-procedure VAT refund should not be deducted from the travel budget.

Retail tax refunds for eligible goods remain a separate system, so qualifying skincare shopping at participating stores may still receive tourist tax treatment. That does not make a clinic procedure refundable.

The best protection is not chasing the lowest headline number. Compare identical quantities, request the complete price in writing, plan for downtime and choose the clinic model that fits your communication and continuity needs.
