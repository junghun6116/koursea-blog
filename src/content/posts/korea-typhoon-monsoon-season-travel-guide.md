Korea's rainy monsoon period is commonly concentrated in June and July, while typhoon impacts are more common in late summer. Conditions vary by year, so use live official notices rather than a fixed itinerary assumption.

## Before leaving your hotel

Check the [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/index.do) warning map, your airline's flight status and [KORAIL](https://www.letskorail.com/) service notices. Save screenshots of bookings and keep an indoor alternative near your accommodation.

Use a simple three-check sequence: weather authority, transport operator, then the attraction or local authority responsible for the place you plan to visit. A normal train does not prove that a coastal trail is open, and an open attraction does not prove that your flight is operating.

## If a flight or train is disrupted

Follow the operating carrier's current rebooking, refund or compensation instructions. There is no single rule that covers every airline, ticket type or weather event; ask the carrier or KORAIL directly and keep the notice that caused the change.

## A practical backup day

Choose museums, department stores, cafes or covered markets close to a subway station. Leave buffer time for walking between transfers, and never enter a closed trail, shore or river park during a warning.

Keep the backup in the same district or on one direct subway line. Moving across the city for an indoor attraction can create more exposure at street-level transfers and station exits than staying near the hotel. Check the attraction's own notice before travelling; severe weather can close an indoor venue as well.

## Official sources

- [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/index.do)
- [KORAIL](https://www.letskorail.com/)
- [Incheon Airport flight status](https://www.airport.kr/ap/en/dep/depPasSchList.do)

## Read the alert, not only the forecast icon

A rain symbol does not tell you whether a trail, river park, ferry or flight is affected. Check the warning text, affected area and valid time on the KMA notice. Then check the operator that controls the part of the trip you are taking: the airline for a flight, KORAIL for an intercity train, and the local authority or attraction for a closure.

Do not use an old screenshot as proof that a service is running. Refresh the notice before departure and again before a long transfer. If an operator cancels a service, keep the cancellation notice and booking confirmation together for any refund or insurance claim.

## Match the notice to the part of Korea you are visiting

Warnings are issued for specific areas and periods. Read the affected region and valid time instead of assuming that one Seoul forecast describes Busan, Jeju or a mountain destination. If your itinerary crosses regions, check both the departure and arrival areas plus any ferry, bridge or rail operator used between them.

For airport days, use [Incheon Airport's live departure information](https://www.airport.kr/ap/en/dep/depPasSchList.do) and the airline's own status page. An airport board can show the operational state, while the airline remains the controlling source for rebooking and ticket conditions.

## Pack for a flexible day

Protect passports, medication and charging equipment in a waterproof pouch. Wear shoes with grip and avoid flooded underpasses, river paths, shorelines and mountain trails during warnings. A useful backup plan stays within the same part of the city so it does not create another long, weather-exposed journey.

Download the booking details and hotel address before leaving reliable Wi-Fi, and keep a charging cable or power bank accessible rather than buried in checked luggage. When the forecast is unstable, avoid scheduling a non-refundable intercity move immediately before an international flight.
